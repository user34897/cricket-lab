About Cricket Lab

A personal cricket stats site, built entirely from free, open ball-by-ball data.

Where the data comes from

Every number here is worked out from Cricsheet, which publishes ball-by-ball records of cricket matches. The data is used under the Open Data Commons Attribution License (ODC-By 1.0). Thank you to Cricsheet for making it available. Cricket Lab is not affiliated with Cricsheet.

The site refreshes itself once a week, so new matches appear a few days after Cricsheet adds them.

Competitions, kept separate

Men’s Tests, ODIs, T20 internationals, the Big Bash League (BBL) and the Indian Premier League (IPL). No women’s cricket. Each competition is built from its own Cricsheet download into its own set of files, so no figure ever mixes competitions: a Test average only counts Tests, an IPL strike rate only IPL matches. Player pages show each competition separately and never add them together.

T20 internationals have two views: full member nations only (the default: matches where both teams are one of the 12 full ICC members) and all teams, which adds the many matches between smaller cricket nations.

Career figures are not complete careers. Cricsheet’s ball-by-ball records only go back so far and some matches are missing, so totals can be lower than official records. Each player page links to their official ESPNcricinfo profile.

Afghanistan

Cricsheet no longer publishes matches involving Afghanistan’s men’s team (see Cricsheet’s notes in each download). Their players’ league matches, such as the IPL and BBL, are still included. So:

  • On Test, ODI and T20I views, Afghanistan players are marked: Afghanistan: internationals missing. Their IPL and BBL records show normally.
  • Every other team’s international matches against Afghanistan are missing too, so players who faced Afghanistan have a few matches fewer than their official records, and head-to-heads against Afghanistan players can’t be shown. These aren’t marked individually, because Cricsheet doesn’t say who played in the withheld matches.

How we know who’s an Afghanistan player: we keep a list of every player capped by Afghanistan, taken from ESPNcricinfo’s official Afghanistan Test, ODI and T20I caps lists, plus Wikidata (the open database behind Wikipedia). Players are matched by their ESPNcricinfo ID, which Cricsheet’s player register records, so there’s no guessing from names (Afghanistan’s Zahir Khan isn’t mixed up with India’s Zaheer Khan, for example). Each weekly build also asks Wikidata for any newly listed players.

Which years are incomplete?

How we work it out. Tests, ODIs and T20Is all have official match numbers, in the order the matches were played. Cricsheet records each match’s number, so lining its matches up in number order shows the gaps. For Tests, every gap is treated as a missing Test. For ODIs and T20Is a gap can also be a match abandoned without a ball being bowled (these still get a number) or a withheld Afghanistan match, so we cross-check with Cricsheet’s list of missing matches, which gives each missing match’s date and teams. The BBL and IPL have no official numbers, so for them we use that list directly.

  • Tests: everyone who played during the patchy early years is marked (same as before).
  • ODIs: everyone who played before the first year Cricsheet has 90% of matches is marked; after that, only players from the teams in each missing match.
  • T20Is: full-member T20Is look complete (the gaps between them are abandoned or withheld matches), but Cricsheet doesn’t have every match between smaller nations. So in the all teams view, players from non-full-member teams are marked in years with such gaps.
  • BBL / IPL: players from the teams in each missing match are marked. Cricsheet lists no missing IPL matches.

A badge means a record may be incomplete, not that it definitely is.

How the numbers are worked out

Batting averageRuns divided by the number of times out.
Strike rate (batting)Runs per 100 balls faced. Wides don’t count as balls faced; no-balls do. In white-ball cricket it matters as much as the average, so it’s shown up front.
Bowling averageRuns conceded per wicket. Byes and leg byes aren’t charged to the bowler.
EconomyRuns conceded per six-ball over.
Strike rate (bowling)Balls bowled per wicket.
Nervous ninetiesOf the innings where a batter reached 90, the share where they were out before 100.
50 → 100 conversionOf all innings of 50 or more, the share that became 100 or more.
Dot ball %The share of balls faced that the batter scored nothing off (no-balls aren’t counted as dots).
PhasesPowerplay, middle and death overs: which over of the innings each ball was bowled in.
SeasonsThe BBL and IPL are grouped by season (a BBL season runs December to February, e.g. 2023/24); internationals by calendar year.
Not out“Retired hurt” and “retired not out” count as not out.

Names

Players are listed the way Cricsheet names them: initials, then surname (“SPD Smith” is Steve Smith). The search understands first names too, so “Steve Smith” finds “SPD Smith”. It searches every competition at once.
